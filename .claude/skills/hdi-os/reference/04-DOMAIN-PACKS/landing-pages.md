# Domain Pack — Marketing and Landing Pages

## Primary outcome

Help a specific audience understand relevance, trust the promise, see credible proof, resolve risk, and take the intended action.

## Dominant priorities

1. Message hierarchy.
2. Audience recognition.
3. Differentiated point of view.
4. Product or service proof.
5. Narrative pacing.
6. Conversion clarity.
7. Brand memorability.
8. Mobile and loading performance.

## Required architecture

A strong page usually answers, in an evidence-based order:

- Is this for me?
- What problem or ambition does it recognize?
- What is the point of view or method?
- What exactly is offered?
- Can I see it working?
- Is it suitable for my situation?
- What does setup, use, or purchase require?
- What does it cost or what is the next commercial step?
- What objections remain?
- What should I do now?

Do not force a fixed template. One section should have one dominant communication job.

## Visual behavior

- Use section rhythm, not repeated feature-card blocks.
- Keep one dominant focal idea per viewport.
- Make product proof readable and real.
- Use display typography strategically; body copy remains comfortable.
- Let visual concept express the brand, not merely a trendy effect.
- Reserve strongest contrast for promise, proof, and action.

## Conversion rules

- Primary CTA describes the next step honestly.
- Secondary CTA serves a distinct intent.
- Risk-reduction copy must be true.
- Forms ask only what is needed for the current step.
- Repeat CTAs at moments of readiness, not after every section.
- Do not invent urgency, scarcity, customer counts, or testimonials.

## Product proof

- Use real screenshots, data, process evidence, case studies, or demonstrations.
- Keep screenshots large enough to understand.
- Explain what the viewer should notice.
- Separate current capability from roadmap.

## Motion

- Use motion to sequence the story, demonstrate cause and effect, or reveal product behavior.
- Do not apply the same fade-up to every section.
- Heavy scroll scenes need static mobile and reduced-motion versions.

## Mobile

- Preserve promise, proof, and action in the initial experience.
- Recompose split heroes.
- Keep text measure and CTA reach comfortable.
- Replace tiny device collages with focused proof.
- Avoid excessive vertical stacking of decorative content.

## Common failures

- Generic centered SaaS hero.
- Vague headline followed by feature jargon.
- Three equal cards repeated across the page.
- Fake product UI or unreadable screenshots.
- Beautiful visuals with no conversion sequence.
- Every section using the same background, spacing, and composition.
- Long desktop story that becomes an exhausting mobile stack.
- Pricing or trial claims without product truth.

## Recommended skill order

`design-director` → `reference-intelligence` → `ux-architecture` → `visual-composition` → `typography-content` → `color-imagery` → `motion-interaction` → `responsive-rtl` → `design-to-code` → `visual-qa-refinement`

## Additional hard gates

- [ ] The primary audience and promise are clear in the initial viewport.
- [ ] The main CTA is visible, specific, and truthful.
- [ ] Product/service proof is legible and sourced.
- [ ] Section order follows the buyer's decision, not a generic template.
- [ ] Mobile preserves conversion without requiring the full visual spectacle.
