SPARKLE MODULE 03 — PRODUCTION BRIEF EXAMPLES

Production-ready brief patterns — Sparkle Auto

These are authored examples for a production session, not already-generated images or
human-approved new scenes. Resolve bracketed inputs and establish any required project anchor
before making a tool call. No user-supplied example image is required.
Keep parameters compatible with the actual tool.

The comprehensive brief lives in the project record; the compact instructions below
are what the image tool needs. Do not paste the entire design system into each call.

Shared identity/style block

Use after resolving the locked identity specification and, when available, the accepted internal identity anchor:

```text
Use the locked Sparkle Owner identity specification and the currently accepted project
identity anchor when the tool supports it. Preserve his facial structure, swept hair,
approved facial-hair treatment, body proportions, navy overshirt, plain white T-shirt,
beige trousers, navy sneakers, and dark smartwatch on the locked anatomical wrist.
Do not redesign his face or wardrobe between scenes.

If no identity anchor exists yet, stop batch production and create one calibration asset
from this specification first. Validate it, correct it, and mark the accepted output as
the project anchor before producing recurring-character scenes.

Use the selected premium stylized 3D rendering family with soft controlled studio
light, coherent contact shadows, restrained realistic fabric detail, and clean edges.
Graphic objects use Sparkle Blue #01A2FF, Dark Blue #0C264B, Deep Navy #03132E, pale
blue, and white. Preserve natural skin/hair and the approved beige clothing.
No text, logos, UI screens, charts, price labels, sticker clutter, or extra characters.
```

Do not claim details are locked unless the project identity specification actually defines them.
When a detail is undefined, either preserve the accepted calibration anchor consistently or record the
decision before batch production. Keep the final tool instruction concise once the identity block is stable.

A. Problem — “The wash owner should not be the call center”

Section: problem. Goal: recognition of the burden of every question returning
to one person. Visual verb: pulls. Main prop: one oversized telephone receiver.
Owner: required and actively involved, not pointing at the viewer.

Page composition: short Arabic copy outside the art, normally on the right.
Art occupies the opposite slot; do not bake a text-safe blank half into the cutout.
Desktop: full action and feet if useful; a compact curved cable, not a background vortex.
Mobile: retain expression, grip, and receiver; simplify/recompose the cable.
Product proof: no product UI in this scene; the next section supplies real proof.
Layers: Owner+receiver+gripping/contact elements as one coherent alpha group;
optional contact shadow if it can be extracted cleanly; simple directional spark in code.

Tool-ready scene instructions, appended to the shared block:

```text
Create an isolated website story asset, not a webpage. The Sparkle Owner is being
pulled sideways by the weight of one oversized telephone receiver that he is holding
with a believable two-handed grip. His stance is mildly braced and his expression
shows relatable operational overload, not panic or slapstick humiliation. The receiver
is the only dominant prop, with one short controlled curve of cable. Make the action
legible through silhouette and scale contrast. Keep generous safe edges around the
head, hands, shoes, and receiver; no floating question cards or papers. Compose the
action to face toward [the actual copy side]. Use a transparent background when
supported; otherwise follow the explicitly selected clean-plate workflow.
```

Acceptance: one-second meaning; same Owner; physical grip/feet; one metaphor; no
unwanted lettering; alpha verified; 390 px composition still communicates the action.

B. Hero — “Control without standing at the branch”

Goal: support the primary promise without overpowering real product proof.
Visual verb: steps away. Prop: one simplified car-wash-bay doorway outline.
Owner: optional. If the real UI needs the space, omit this scene entirely.

HTML: headline, support, review CTA, product frame, approved logo. REAL: current
owner dashboard and/or actual booking view. Generated art: only the story group.
Do not generate a phone/laptop screen or a nonexistent “remote control” feature.

```text
Create a restrained isolated story asset. The approved Sparkle Owner is taking one
relaxed step out through a minimal stylized car-wash-bay doorway, suggesting freedom
from having to stand at the branch all day. Keep the doorway simple, unbranded, and
secondary; this is an editorial metaphor, not a representation of an actual branch.
He looks naturally toward [the actual product-panel side], with no presenter gesture,
no thumbs-up, and no device in his hands. Use the locked render style, wardrobe,
and lighting. Leave clear silhouette separation and enough safe edge room. Do not
include a dashboard, phone screen, headline, CTA, signage, ribbons, or extra props.
```

Integration: keep the doorway/Owner behind or beside the separate real product frame;
no face or important UI obscured. Avoid a tiny whole-dashboard collage on mobile.
If the metaphor distracts from proof, reduce or omit it instead of making it bigger.

C. Final CTA — resolution of the same operating pressure

Goal: provide an earned emotional close. Visual verb: releases. Continuity:
returns to the telephone metaphor from the problem, now supported and under control.
Message: operations no longer have to sit on the owner’s shoulders; not a promise
that the software eliminates all calls.

```text
Using the locked Sparkle Owner identity and the accepted problem-scene telephone design,
create a calm resolution asset. The receiver now rests securely on one simple low
cradle, and the Owner has just released his grip and is stepping back with relaxed
shoulders and a small natural smile. The telephone is stable and orderly; the Owner
is acting in the scene, not pointing or giving a sales presentation. Preserve the
same render family, material language, camera logic, and wardrobe. Use a clean cutout
that remains readable on the selected navy or white section surface. No text, UI,
buttons, badges, extra characters, or decorative particle field.
```

Do not darken the subject arbitrarily for a navy surface; inspect edge separation and
actual lighting. Build the conversion message and buttons in a stable HTML column.
A reused compatible approved asset is preferable to a needless new scene.

D. Setup — one small branch prop, no Owner

Goal: make setup feel manageable. Main prop: a simplified blank car-wash-bay
model. Use: only if it improves comprehension beside the real three setup steps.

```text
Create one small premium stylized 3D car-wash-bay model for a website setup section.
Use a simple open bay, clean navy/white geometry, and one controlled Sparkle Blue
accent. Match the selected site's camera angle, material softness, and light. Keep
it recognizable at a small display size, with no tiny signage, readable text, logos,
charts, people, complex environment, or ornamental splash. Output an isolated asset
with safe edges and a clean contact shadow using the supported background workflow.
```

The model is editorial illustration, not a photograph of a real customer location.
Setup numbers/labels remain in HTML. A useful simple SVG or existing approved prop may
replace generation; the skill’s job is the best visual decision, not maximum tool calls.

E. Trial — blank calendar object, terms in HTML

Goal: visualize a bounded evaluation period. Terms: verify before publishing.
Owner: omitted to preserve rhythm. Main prop: one blank calendar stand.

```text
Create a clean premium stylized 3D calendar stand as a compact supporting website
asset. Use white surfaces, a navy structural edge, and one blue binding detail in the
same material/light family as the approved Sparkle assets. The calendar face is blank,
with no numbers, date grid, text, logo, checkmarks, plan name, or price. Keep the form
simple and readable, with safe edges and a coherent shadow. It must support rather
than compete with the adjacent HTML trial explanation.
```

Do not render “14” into the asset. The number/terms belong in verified live text;
placing HTML over a blank prop is optional and must remain readable/responsive.
Do not promise an automatic free trial without the maintained review/eligibility flow.

Mobile-variant instruction

```text
Recompose the accepted selected scene for its actual mobile art slot. Preserve the
same character identity, single metaphor, main action, light, and prop design.
Prioritize face, hands/contact, and dominant prop; remove only the nonessential
secondary area identified in the brief. Do not squash the full desktop composition,
mirror the character, invent a new pose without need, or add labels.
```

Prefer a safe deterministic crop of the approved asset when it preserves the message;
use generation for a genuine recomposition, not every file-size derivative.

Generalization beyond Sparkle

Do not reuse the above blue Owner scenes for every client. Keep the method: one user
question, one visual verb, one metaphor, truthful proof, code-built interaction, and
layout-specific production. A different brand may call for a material study, an original
architectural vignette, a restrained photographic scene, or typography with no character.
